I don't believe that rotation or orientation of the object should be considered semantic detail. A guitar rotated 45 degrees is still a guitar.

As for the number of subjects, I myself am unclear on whether such a detail should be considered spurious or semantic. My intuition is that, in an image with multiple items, if one item can be discerned to be the primary subject of the piece, then the semantic rationale should revolve around that one subject, as though it were alone in the image.

I noticed a focus on the background of the images and the possible context. For example, in 04, the music notes are taken as possibly suggesting music or performance. I think it's good that such things are noticed, because they change the semantic meaning of an object from a guitar to a guitar being played. However, it's important to put focus on defining the image around the subject, not its background. Having flowers behind a guitar in one image and having no flowers behind a guitar in another does not change the fact that it's a guitar. Of course, it's a hard line to draw, and I'm not saying that all background elements should be ignored (for example, a human holding a guitar vs a guitar without a human could be semantically different), but just take note of the importance of focusing on the subject and the state of the subject for "semantics", not the surrounding elements.

I think that, POTENTIALLY, a good rule of thumb is asking "could these two images be of the same guitar instance, regardless of style, color, orientation, cropping, or other spurious change?". Although, that leads to more questions because while different elephants could plausibly be argued to be the same, different humans or different guitars are much easier to tell apart, at least through the eyes of a human validator.
